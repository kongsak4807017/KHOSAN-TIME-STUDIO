# Creative Constitution

## Purpose

The Creative Constitution protects a game's recognizable point of view while allowing implementation to learn rapidly.

It is created before expensive production and becomes a human-controlled boundary.

## Suggested sections

### 1. Player fantasy
Who does the player get to be?

### 2. Emotional promise
What recurring feelings should the game produce?

### 3. Core verbs
What does the player repeatedly do?

### 4. World point of view
What does this game believe or examine about its world?

### 5. Art constitution
Define a small visual grammar:
- shape language,
- proportion,
- color grammar,
- material vocabulary,
- architecture vocabulary,
- camera language,
- lighting identity,
- animation personality,
- UI personality.

The goal is not “beautiful assets.” The goal is visual recognizability across thousands of generated assets.

### 6. Mechanical identity
Rules or relationships that make the game itself, not merely its genre.

Example:
> Every supernatural power changes what the world remembers about the player.

### 7. Sacred constraints
Things community requests or optimization work must not silently remove.

### 8. Flexible implementation
Areas that may change freely in response to evidence.

### 9. Audience hypothesis
Who may love the game, and why? This is a hypothesis, not demographic truth.

### 10. Falsification
What evidence would make the team reconsider the concept?

## Locking

A Creative Constitution has lifecycle states:

~~~text
draft -> prototype-tested -> human-locked -> amended
~~~

Material amendments require HG-01 and an evolution record explaining:
- evidence,
- alternatives,
- what remains invariant,
- expected effect,
- experiment/verification plan.

## Rule

A live-service team may respond to community pressure without changing the Constitution. If the proposed solution changes a sacred constraint or emotional promise, it is a vision amendment, not a balance patch.
